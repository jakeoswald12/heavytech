HeavyTechMarket Study

The compact track loader is the single best-selling construction machine in the United States, and it is still growing. Almost none of it is electrified. HeavyTech's opening is real, but it is narrower and slower than a battery-electric pitch suggests. The evidence points to a hybrid-first CTL, sold through rental and a few service partners, funded in part by technology licensing and engineering work.

[ 01 ] / EXEC SUMMARY

What we found

  1. F1

    The CTL is the right first machine

    CTLs are the top-selling US construction category: 56,157 new financed units in the 12 months to April 2026, up 1.2% while most categories fell S·H1. North American CTL sales grew from about 21,500 (2012) to about 93,000 (2022) as they displaced skid steers S·M9. We size US CTL + skid-steer new sales at $5.5–9.5B a year E·M.

  2. F2

    Electric demand is tiny today

    About 250 electric construction machines sold in North America in 2023, roughly 0.07% of the market, and Off-Highway Research expects only about 1% (≈3,000 units) by 2028 S·H16. In 2023 just 100 electric CTLs were sold in NA, all to rental companies S·H14. Globally the picture is different: electric sales doubled to 37,276 units in 2025, mostly in China S·M19.

  3. F3

    Price is the wall, not physics

    Electric compact machines sell at 2.5–3× the diesel price S·M16. Bobcat's T7X only reaches diesel-like pricing in California with a $210,000 state voucher S·H25. Battery packs averaged $108/kWh in 2025 S·H24, so cells are not what makes these machines expensive; low volume and system engineering are D·M. A hybrid HT-1 has to land near diesel price to sell without subsidy.

  4. F4

    The hybrid CTL slot is open

    Bobcat, Volvo, CASE, New Holland and Takeuchi now sell or show battery-electric compacts in the US. We found no hybrid compact track loader on the US market, and no verified electric CTL from Kubota, the CTL share leader at 27.1% S·H1. That gap is the opening, and it will not stay open for long.

  5. F5

    Rental is the gatekeeper

    Rental firms bought 33% of the 333,000 machines sold in North America in 2023, and about 80% of NA rental revenue goes to publicly listed companies S·H1415. Rental fleets do not capture fuel savings, which weakens the TCO pitch S·M16. They do respond to jobsite mandates, noise rules and utilization, so the pitch to them must be about rentability.

  6. F6

    Right-to-repair is a wedge

    The FTC's July 2026 settlement forces Deere to open repair tools for 10 years, but it covers agricultural equipment only S·H48. Recent state digital repair laws in NY, OR and MN exclude off-road equipment, and dealer groups are pushing the same exemption elsewhere S·M4950. In construction, open repair is a voluntary differentiator HeavyTech can own.

  7. F7

    Defense is a side door

    The Army cancelled the Robotic Combat Vehicle award in 2025 and reset it toward cheaper commercial robots with a reported $650,000 unit cap S·M5758. S-MET Increment II could reach 2,195 units, but two incumbents already hold prototype contracts S·H5556. SBIR money (Phase I up to $323,090, Phase II up to $2.15M) is the realistic near-term defense revenue S·M59.

Recommendation

Hybrid first, rental-led

Lead with the HT-1 as a diesel-electric hybrid CTL priced within roughly 15–25% of a comparable diesel machine E·L, and treat the battery-electric pack as a California and indoor option. Validate it in 2027 with three to five lighthouse fleets and one regional rental company, while selling the electro-hydraulic and ORIGIN technology to an established OEM as paid engineering work or a license. Do not build a dealer network before you have field reliability data; sign two or three independent multi-line dealers as service partners instead. Make open diagnostics a written, contractual promise, because in construction nobody is forced to make it. Pursue defense only through SBIR and commercial-robot solicitations, not as a primary market.

Drivers that apply
  • CARB's in-use off-road rule phases out Tier 0 and Tier 1 engines between 2024 and 2030 and required renewable diesel from 2024 S·H43. EPA granted CARB an amendment authorization for it in 2026 44.
  • California's Clean Construction list, effective 1 Jan 2026, expects compact track and wheel loaders and mini-ex up to 15,000 lb to be zero-emission for project recognition S·H45.
  • CORE vouchers up to $500,000 for zero-emission construction equipment S·H46.
What does not apply
  • CORE and the Clean Construction list reward zero-emission machines. A diesel-electric HT-1 would not qualify; only a battery-electric configuration would D·M.
  • We found no current federal purchase incentive for off-road compact equipment. Treat any such claim with caution until verified.
  • Tier 4 Final already applies to every new diesel; it raises cost but does not force electrification.
Barriers
  • Purchase price 2.5–3× diesel; a 2023 Kiloutou study found 2.5 t electric mini-ex lifetime TCO still 5–10% above diesel in France S·M47.
  • Charging: fleets returning at shift end need site power, not one plug (Andrew Johnson's own framing).
  • Rental buyers do not keep fuel savings 16.

[ 05 ] / COMPETITION

Who else is building

Five major OEMs sell or show battery-electric compact machines in the US. No hybrid CTL is on the market. Kubota and Cat, the top two CTL brands, have no verified electric CTL.

What this means for HeavyTech's architecture

Incumbents have taken three routes to electric work functions. Bobcat removed hydraulics entirely, using ball-screw actuators for lift and tilt 29. Volvo and New Holland use one dedicated electric motor for the whole hydraulic circuit 3034. HeavyTech's one-motor-per-pump, valveless design sits between them. Suppliers already sell the building blocks: Danfoss (Editron, plus the hydraulics business it bought from Eaton), Bosch Rexroth eLION, Parker's electric motors and inverters, and Terzo's smart electro-hydraulic pumps 404142. The defensible part is therefore the controls software, the patent-pending system layout and ORIGIN, not the hardware.

Autonomy is being funded elsewhere

Bedrock Robotics raised a $270M Series B in February 2026 at a $1.75B valuation, with more than $350M raised in total, to retrofit existing excavators for autonomy 38. Built Robotics, Teleo and SafeAI also sell retrofits 39. HeavyTech should not compete on autonomy software. An autonomy-ready, open machine is a better position: one that Bedrock-style companies can integrate with, and that rental fleets can buy without locking in a single autonomy vendor.

What would make a rental company adopt a new brand
  • A demand pull it can bill forCustomers who ask for a zero-emission or near-silent machine and pay a higher rate. In 2023 every electric CTL in NA went to rental 14, and Bobcat launched the T7X through Sunbelt in California 28.
  • De-risked residual valueA buyback or guaranteed residual for the first fleet. Without used-price history, rental finance teams will discount an unknown brand heavily E·M.
  • Uptime supportParts within a day, remote diagnostics into the fleet's telematics system, and techs who can work on it. Open diagnostics and commercial parts directly answer this.
  • Familiar controls and attachmentsUniversal quick-attach and standard joystick patterns so renters need no training.
Right-to-repair as a competitive edge
  • FederalFTC and five states settled with Deere on 8 July 2026. For 10 years Deere must give owners and independent shops the same diagnostic software, manuals and part-pairing tools its dealers get. The order covers agricultural equipment S·H48. Deere also paid $99M in an April 2026 class settlement.
  • StatesColorado has an agricultural repair law; New York, Oregon and Minnesota digital repair laws exclude off-road equipment, according to dealer testimony S·M50. AEM and AED opposed 2026 Rhode Island bills unless off-road is exempted 49.
  • ImplicationConstruction buyers have no legal guarantee of repair access, so a written, enforceable HeavyTech commitment (open diagnostics, published parts cross-references, source and firmware escrow for fleets) is a real differentiator. It also undercuts incumbent parts margins, which is exactly why incumbents won't copy it quickly.

Dealer economics

Equipment dealers make little on new iron. Farm-equipment dealer surveys say a sales department needs 5–7% margin on wholegoods to cover itself, and that the business is carried by parts and service S·M54. The average North American dealer covered about 73% of fixed costs from parts and service gross profit, against an 80% guideline S·M53. A dealer's incentive is to sell parts. An open-parts machine therefore needs a different dealer deal, such as a larger share on the machine, service labor and a diagnostics subscription.

Dealer laws and direct sales

About 45 states have equipment dealer laws, and a 2016 summary counted 25 that cover construction equipment S·M51. These laws generally require good cause and advance notice (180 days in Texas; 90 days in Louisiana) to terminate a dealer, and require buyback of inventory and parts 52. We found no statute that bars a manufacturer from selling construction equipment directly, unlike car franchise laws E·M. The practical risk is the reverse: once a dealer is signed, exiting is slow and costly.

[ 08 ] / DEFENSE

DefenseX reality check

Defense revenue before 2030 is likely limited to SBIR and prototype work. The programs closest to DefenseX already have incumbent suppliers.

  • S-MET Increment IITwo OTA prototype awards in September 2024 to American Rheinmetall (Mission Master) and HDT (Hunter WOLF, a hybrid-electric 6×6), reported as $22M combined (some outlets say $22M each), eight prototypes each. A production decision is planned for late FY2027, for up to 2,195 systems S·H5556. HeavyTech is too late for this round; the realistic role is supplier or teaming partner.
  • RCV → UGCRVThe Army moved to cancel the Robotic Combat Vehicle award in May 2025 57. It has reportedly been reset as Unmanned Ground Commercial Robotic Vehicles with a $650,000 per-unit cap, run from Warren, Michigan S·M58. Commercial-derivative platforms are the explicit target, which fits DefenseX's pitch. Follow it closely.
  • SBIR / STTRReauthorized on 13 April 2026 through 2031. Phase I up to $323,090 and Phase II up to $2,153,927 without SBA waiver; a new Strategic Breakthrough category goes up to $30M S·M59. This is the most realistic first defense dollar.
  • DIU and OTAsCommercial Solutions Openings and Other Transaction agreements are the fast path for commercial tech; S-MET II itself used OTAs 55. Requires a working prototype and a defense-experienced teammate.
  • Our estimateRealistic defense revenue for HeavyTech: $0.3–3M over 2027–29 from SBIR and prototype work, with production revenue unlikely before 2030 E·L. Do not let DefenseX pull engineering time from HT-1 before Phase 1 gates are met.
[ 09 ] / RISKS

Risks and conditions

  • Capital · HighMachine programs need tens of millions before revenue. Funding through Reg CF and Reg D 506(c) 62 is unlikely to cover tooling, certification and inventory.
  • Price parity · HighIf the HT-1 lands near battery-electric pricing, the addressable market shrinks to the subsidy-driven niche.
  • Incumbent response · HighKubota, Cat or Deere launching a hybrid or lower-cost electric CTL would close the gap. Deere already showed seven electric and hybrid-electric models at CONEXPO 2026 65.
  • Channel · MediumRental fleets won't take an unknown brand without residual guarantees; dealers resist open parts.
  • Technical · MediumValveless multi-motor hydraulics must survive heat, dust and shock loads over thousands of hours; the first prototype was about 80% built as of May 2026 63.
  • Regulatory · MediumHybrids miss zero-emission incentives; federal-state emissions disputes make California's direction uncertain.
  • Supply chain · Low–MedTariffs on Chinese-built electric equipment and components raise costs 16, but also help a US-built product.
What must be true
  1. The hybrid HT-1 sells for no more than about 25% above a comparable diesel CTL and pays back within a typical rental ownership period.
  2. Field data shows uptime at least equal to diesel within the first 10,000 fleet hours.
  3. At least one rental company or OEM pays for the technology before volume manufacturing starts.
  4. Open diagnostics is real and visible: published, contractual, and demonstrated to fleet mechanics.
  5. The company raises enough capital to reach Phase 2 gates without depending on defense revenue.
  6. ORIGIN and the control software become IP that an OEM would license, so HeavyTech is worth something even if machine sales grow slowly.
